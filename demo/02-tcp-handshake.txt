TCP establishes a connection using a three-way handshake. The client sends a SYN segment carrying an initial sequence number. The server replies with SYN-ACK, acknowledging the client's sequence number and supplying its own. The client returns an ACK, and the connection enters the ESTABLISHED state. This exchange costs one full round trip before any application data can be sent, which is why protocols such as QUIC fold the handshake into the TLS exchange. Connections are closed with a four-way handshake using FIN and ACK segments, and the initiating side then waits in TIME_WAIT for twice the maximum segment lifetime to ensure delayed duplicates cannot be misread as belonging to a new connection.
